Case Study Synopsis

Same structure as the original, refreshed for today's markets: digital and omnichannel strategy, data and AI, sustainability, and a wider toolkit of analysis frameworks. Teal boxes say what to do. Amber boxes mark what is new compared with the original.

Section 1

Introduction

Do: In one short paragraph, name the company, its industry, size, location and business model, then say why the case matters now (a disruption, a growth chance, a shift in customer behaviour).
  • Company snapshot: founded, revenue or scale, core offer, channels (stores, online, marketplace, subscription).
  • The trigger: what changed that forces a decision?
  • A one-line preview of the report's path from problem to recommendation.
Section 2

Problem statement

Do: Write one question that is broad enough to leave strategy options open but measurable enough to judge success.

Formula: How can [company] [achieve objective] among [target] by [timeframe], measured by [KPIs]?

Example: How can a regional grocery chain grow online revenue 25% and lift repeat-purchase rate to 45% within 24 months without reducing store-level profit margin?

New: Use SMART or OKR-style targets and include at least one metric beyond sales and share: customer lifetime value (CLV), customer acquisition cost (CAC), Net Promoter Score (NPS), retention, or a sustainability target. These become the scoring criteria in Section 8.
Section 3

Key findings, facts and assumptions

Do: List only the facts that bear on the main problem and are not covered later in SWOT, PESTEL or competition. Label each as fact (sourced) or assumption (your judgment).
  • Market size and growth rate, with the year and source.
  • Recent company performance trends (3 to 5 years).
  • Shifts in buying behaviour: e-commerce share, mobile use, social commerce, subscription, resale.
  • Constraints: budget, regulation, supply chain, talent, technology debt.
New: Prefer sources from the last 2 to 3 years. Good places to look: Statistics Canada, Statista, IBISWorld, Euromonitor, industry associations, company filings (SEDAR+ or SEC), Google Trends, and Think with Google.

Section 5

Internal analysis

5A. SWOT (company level)

Be comprehensive: aim for 5 to 8 points per quadrant, each with evidence and an "so what." Strengths and weaknesses are internal; opportunities and threats come from Section 4.

Strengths

Brand equity, proprietary data, supply chain, talent, cash position, loyalty base, technology stack.

Weaknesses

Legacy systems, channel gaps, dependence on few customers or suppliers, weak digital capability, thin margins.

Opportunities

Trends and gaps from PESTEL, underserved segments, weakened competitors, partnerships, new markets.

Threats

Strong forces from the Five Forces, regulation, economic shocks, disruptive entrants, shifting preferences.

New: TOWS matrix. Turn the SWOT into strategy by pairing quadrants: SO (use strengths to seize opportunities), WO (fix weaknesses using opportunities), ST (use strengths to blunt threats), WT (defend or exit). List 1 to 2 strategic moves per pair. These become your options in Section 8.

5B. VRIO resource test (optional, strong for advantage claims)

For each key resource, ask: is it Valuable, Rare, hard to Imitate, and does the firm have the Organization to exploit it? Only resources that pass all four support a lasting competitive advantage.

New tools to illustrate this section:
  • Perceptual map: plot brands on two axes customers care about (for example price vs. perceived quality) to find open space.
  • Strategy canvas (Blue Ocean): chart how firms score on competing factors, then use Eliminate / Reduce / Raise / Create to find uncontested positioning.
  • Strategic group map: cluster rivals by similar strategy to see who you really compete with.
  • Digital benchmark: compare web traffic, social following and review ratings.

Section 9

Solution

Do: State the strategy in one or two sentences using marketing terminology, tied to your measurable objective.

Example: Pursue market penetration and omnichannel integration to raise online revenue share to 25% within 24 months by investing in a loyalty app, personalized CRM marketing and click-and-collect at existing stores.

Advantages

  1. Strongest link to the problem and KPIs.
  2. Builds on identified strengths or VRIO resources.
  3. Addresses a key threat or force from Section 4.

Disadvantages

  1. Largest cost or resource commitment.
  2. Biggest execution or timing risk.
  3. Dependency on external factors.

Rationale: Explain in 3 to 4 sentences why this option scored highest and why the disadvantages are manageable.

New: Add a short risk table and a contingency trigger.

Timeline, budget and measurement

  • Phases: Quarter 1 foundation; Quarters 2 to 3 launch and test; Quarter 4 onward scale and optimize.
  • Budget: itemize costs, expected return and feasibility.
  • KPIs: tie each tactic to a metric from your problem statement.
  • Review rhythm: monthly dashboard, quarterly strategy review, A/B testing before scaling spend.
New: Address data and ethics and a sustainability or ESG angle.
Section 11

References

Do: List every source you cite, in APA 7th edition.
  • Use in-text citations throughout, not only a final list.
  • Prefer sources from the last 3 years for market data.
  • Cite the actual framework authors where you rely on them.
  • Check each database or website entry includes the retrieval URL or DOI.
